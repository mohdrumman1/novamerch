NovaMerch growth plan

A simple 90-day plan to bring in more club and business orders, while protecting the brand and keeping outreach responsible.

The goal: turn the completed 570-shirt Cooks Hill United FC order into local proof, win more profitable Newcastle and Maitland orders, then expand carefully to Sydney.

How this scales without becoming messy

The system does the repeatable admin. A person makes the important calls: what to recommend, what to promise and when an order is safe to proceed.

1. One clear path in

A local referral, Google search, social post or small ad takes people to one relevant quote form.

2. Lead is captured and owned

The form creates an Airtable lead, records where it came from and gives one person a same-day follow-up task.

3. The right solution is prepared

We ask only for the essentials: purpose, quantity, budget, deadline, artwork and delivery location. Then we send a shortlist, mock-up and quote.

4. Delivery is managed, not guessed

Before promising a date, confirm stock, decoration, supplier production and shipping. After approval, track artwork, production, dispatch and delivery in one board.

5. Each order creates the next one

At delivery, ask for a review, case-study permission and two introductions. Save the approved specification for reorders.

Human-light rule: prepare one weekly content batch, approve it once, then automate formatting, resizing, scheduling, repurposing and reporting. A person only steps in for identity verification, customer permissions, prices, delivery promises, complaints and real buying conversations.

What we can borrow from the linked video

The video is about how service businesses create repeatable client acquisition. We are adapting the useful ideas for a local merchandise business, not copying every tactic.

  • Productise the first step: sell a free mock-up pack, not a vague “merchandise service.”
  • Start narrow: clubs, club sponsors and uniform-heavy businesses share useful referral paths.
  • Show the work: record short demonstrations of mock-ups, sponsor packs, sizing and order preparation.
  • Use local proof: make the Cooks Hill result visible with permission.
  • Ask for introductions: request two relevant referrals after a successful order or positive check-in.
  • Reply quickly: acknowledge enquiries the same business day, while only promising dates after supplier confirmation.

We will not copy: scraped lists, disguised newsletters, unsolicited directory outreach, mass DMs or notification tricks.

Our 90-day roadmap

Week 1 - get ready

Use one clear contact identity, prepare the Cooks Hill story, set up a simple quote page, and create one delivery board with clear order milestones.

Week 2 - build local paths

Make a list of 30 local referral partners. Test that a form reaches Airtable, gets an owner and creates a same-day task. Test that replies, bounces and unsubscribe requests stop future contact.

Weeks 3–4 - show up locally

Ask for introductions, publish helpful content, request reviews, and attend a local business or club event.

Days 31–60 - test and learn

Run one small advertising test. Publish more proof. Find the audience and offer that produce real quote requests, profitable margins and deliverable work.

Days 61–90 - repeat what works

Put more effort into the best local channel and build club-sponsor partnerships. Only increase outreach once the system is proven safe and accurate.

After 90 days - expand carefully

Move through the wider Hunter first. Test one Sydney area and one customer type only after local results and delivery capacity are proven.

Simple rules for outreach

Do

  • Contact people through referrals, enquiries, permission-based forms or another recorded approved reason.
  • Make every message useful, personal and easy to stop.
  • Immediately stop future contact when someone replies, opts out or an email bounces.
  • Use Monid to research businesses, not to automatically email them.

Do not

  • Increase email volume just because more email addresses have been found.
  • Automatically move leads into a send list.
  • Use public email addresses as proof that marketing contact is allowed.
  • Measure success by email volume instead of profitable orders.
