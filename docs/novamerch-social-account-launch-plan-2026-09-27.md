# NovaMerch social account launch plan

27 September 2026 · One account per day where practical

## Goal

Create a consistent, secure social presence for NovaMerch that generates local awareness, useful conversations and quote requests in Newcastle and Maitland before expanding toward Sydney.

The goal is not to be active everywhere. The goal is to make every account trustworthy, searchable, visually consistent and connected to one conversion path:

> Send your logo, quantity and deadline for a free mock-up and practical quote.

## Human-light operating model

The default should be **prepare once, automate repeatedly**. Human involvement is reserved for actions that require identity, permission, legal judgement or a real business promise.

| Work | Default owner | Human involvement |
|---|---|---|
| Research weekly topics | Newsjack/OpenSEO/approved search prompts | Review only the shortlist |
| Draft captions and platform variants | ChatGPT or Noah | Approve the weekly batch, not every rewrite |
| Generate concept graphics | ChatGPT image generation or Kie | Approve the first template and exceptions |
| Resize, name and archive assets | Script or design automation | None after the template is tested |
| Schedule approved posts | Meta Business Suite, YouTube Studio, TikTok/Pinterest schedulers or an approved scheduler | Approve the calendar batch |
| Repurpose one source item | Automation workflow | None unless the source contains a customer claim |
| Weekly performance report | n8n/Airtable/admin dashboard | Review exceptions and next actions |
| Customer enquiries | Form → Airtable → owner task → draft reply | Human handles qualified buying conversations |
| Account verification, permissions and recovery | Account owner | Always human |
| Customer logos, testimonials and case studies | Account owner | Written permission required |
| Prices, availability and delivery promises | Account owner | Always human |

The target is one short weekly review, not daily manual posting. A post batch should be approved as a group with a defined date range, channels and content rules.

Both are padded to keep the mark inside platform safe areas;

- mock-up builder URL: `https://novamerchau.com/mockup-builder/`;
- quote/contact URL;
- approved Newcastle/Maitland service-area wording;
- 10 real product/order images;
- 3 real short videos or demonstrations;
- Cooks Hill United FC permission before using its name, logo, people or order photos;
- account register recording URL, owner, recovery method, 2FA status and last review.

Never use an employee's personal email as the only owner. Never share passwords with Noah or another agent. Give agents draft or reporting access only.

### Automation setup before opening accounts

Create these reusable templates first:

1. A profile data sheet containing the approved name, description, URL, phone, service area, CTA and opening hours.
2. A content brief form with topic, audience, proof source, CTA, target channels and factual limits.
3. A caption template that produces a short version, a longer version, hashtags and alt text.
4. An image template with logo-safe space, consistent colours and platform dimensions.
5. A publishing calendar with `Draft`, `Approved`, `Scheduled`, `Published`, `Needs permission` and `Do not publish` states.
6. An asset folder structure: `source`, `concept`, `approved`, `scheduled`, `published`, `rights`.
7. A weekly report that counts posts, reach, link clicks, mock-up requests, qualified enquiries and orders.

After the first batch is reviewed, automation can fill the calendar and schedule future posts without a person rewriting each caption.

## Daily setup plan

### Day 1: Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile is free and can display the business in Google Search and Maps. It is appropriate only if NovaMerch makes in-person contact with customers or visits customers. Use a service area if customers do not visit a staffed storefront.

Minimum steps:

1. Sign in with the NovaMerch business Google account.
2. Add or claim `NovaMerch` using the real-world business name.
3. Select the most accurate primary category.
4. Add Newcastle and Maitland only if genuinely served.
5. Add website, phone, hours and email.
6. Write a factual description under 750 characters.
7. Upload logo, cover image and real product/order photos.
8. Add the mock-up builder and quote links.
9. Verify the profile.
10. Add a second trusted owner or manager.
11. Publish one introductory update and answer every review.

Do not keyword-stuff the business name, use a virtual address, or post customer material without permission.

### Day 2: Instagram Professional

1. Create a Professional Business account.
2. Reserve the same handle across platforms, preferably `@novamerch.au` or `@novamerch`.
3. Use the NovaMerch logo and approved bio.
4. Add website and mock-up builder links.
5. Add contact buttons.
6. Connect Meta Business Suite.
7. Turn on 2FA.
8. Create Highlights: Clubs, Businesses, Mock-ups, Products, Reviews and FAQs.
9. Pin three posts: who NovaMerch helps, the free mock-up offer and one approved project.

### Day 3: Facebook Page and Meta Business Suite

## Launch content system

Use four repeatable content pillars:

| Pillar | Examples | Best channels |
|---|---|---|
| Proof | completed products, approved customer projects, reviews | Google, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn |
| Help | sizing, artwork, quantities, product choices, ordering mistakes | Google, LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook |
| Process | mock-up creation, supplier checks, packing, delivery planning | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube |
| Community | local clubs, sponsors, events and useful introductions | Google, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn |

### Minimum weekly rhythm

- Monday: one practical ordering tip.
- Tuesday: one product or mock-up visual.
- Wednesday: one short process video.
- Thursday: one local club/business insight.
- Friday: one FAQ or customer problem solved.
- Once per month: one longer guide or case-study page.

Repurpose one approved idea across channels, but rewrite the opening for each audience. Do not automatically cross-post identical captions everywhere.

## Image and video production workflow

### Use real images for

- completed customer orders;
- customer logos and uniforms;
- people, clubs, venues and events;
- product quality, decoration and packaging claims;
- delivery or fulfilment proof;
- testimonials and case studies.

### Use generated images for

- concept mock-ups before an order exists;
- generic product compositions;
- background textures and campaign graphics;
- educational diagrams;
- clearly labelled illustrative scenes.

Generated images must never imply that a fictional club, person, event, product colour, customer result or delivery outcome is real. Add “concept mock-up” or “illustrative image” where confusion is possible.

### Recommended production pipeline

1. **Brief:** audience, channel, product, location, message, CTA and factual limits.
2. **Generate:** use ChatGPT image generation or Kie for concept art, mock-ups or campaign graphics.
3. **Check:** logo accuracy, readable text, product plausibility, colours, rights and whether the image could be mistaken for a real customer order.
4. **Adapt:** create platform sizes and a web version with descriptive alt text.
5. **Approve:** human checks the image and caption before publishing.
6. **Record:** save source prompt, generated file, final file, channel, date and rights notes.

Do not use generated imagery to manufacture a testimonial, review, customer photograph or case-study result.

## Content record

Keep one row per asset with:

- asset ID;
- source type: real / generated / customer-supplied;
- prompt or source URL;
- claim level: illustrative / verified / customer-approved;
- image rights and permission status;
- platform versions;
- caption and CTA;
- planned date;
- reviewer;
- published URL;
- performance and follow-up action.

## Minimum approval gates

Before publishing, confirm:

- business name and contact details are accurate;
- the offer is real and currently deliverable;
- any customer, club or logo use is approved;
- pricing, product availability and delivery timing are not invented;
- generated visuals are labelled where needed;
- links work and point to the correct page;
- captions do not make unsupported “best”, “fastest” or guaranteed claims;
- messages and comments have a human owner.

These are the only mandatory publishing gates. Everything else should be automated where the platform allows it:

- account creation and verification: human;
- first profile setup: human once, then saved as a template;
- content batch approval: human once per batch;
- customer permission and sensitive claims: human per item;
- routine formatting, scheduling, reporting and repurposing: automated;
- high-risk comments, complaints, opt-outs and buying questions: human escalation.

### Automated stop rules

Automation must pause a post and mark it `Needs permission` when it detects:

- a customer name, logo, face, testimonial or order quantity;
- a price, delivery date, stock claim or product guarantee;
- words such as `best`, `fastest`, `guaranteed`, `official`, `exclusive` or `number one`;
- an unclear source or generated image that could look like real customer proof;
- a negative comment, complaint, opt-out or request for a quote.

This keeps human review focused on the small number of posts that can create real risk.

## First 30 days after setup

### Week 1

Complete Google, Instagram and Facebook. Publish the introductory posts and verify every link.

### Week 2

Complete LinkedIn and YouTube. Publish one business guide and one product/process video.

### Week 3

Test TikTok with three videos. Set up Pinterest if the image library is ready.

### Week 4

Review profile views, website clicks, mock-up requests, quote requests, qualified opportunities and actual orders. Keep only channels with a clear owner and repeatable content supply.

## Success measures

The main measures are:

- qualified local enquiries;
- mock-up requests;
- quote-to-order conversion;
- profitable order value;
- reviews and permissioned referrals;
- response time;
- content saves, shares and link clicks;
- cost per qualified enquiry where advertising is used.

Follower count is a secondary measure. A smaller local audience that requests quotes is more valuable than a large unrelated audience.
